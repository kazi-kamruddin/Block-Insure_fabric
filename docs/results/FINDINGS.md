## Realistic amounts and medical cover rules — 21 November 2026, night

Source review of every premium, cover, claim, benefit and banking path, with Go and web unit tests and a live synthetic one-host run on LevelDB dev-lite 1.9.0 sequence 7. See the [dev-lite runbook](../dev-lite-medical.md#realistic-amounts-and-cover-rules-on-sequence-7-21-november-2026). All amounts remain simulated BDT. The rules apply to medical packages (`MedicalRulesVersion` 1); other products keep their recorded behaviour, and policies issued before sequence 7 keep their issued terms.

| ID | Finding | Scope | Evidence and disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F24 | The rehearsals and demo presets used amounts of BDT 5 to BDT 950, far below both Bangladeshi hospital prices and the frozen XGBoost adapter's scale (one model unit is BDT 100,000). Model advice on those inputs said little about realistic claims. | Source review, offline paired cases | Fixed in rehearsals, presets and placeholders. The product is BDT 1,250 per 30 days, with BDT 300,000 of cover, a BDT 150,000 surgery sublimit, a 10% deductible capped at BDT 5,000, and an 80% insurer share. A routine BDT 85,000 bill pays BDT 64,000. The frozen weights, threshold 0.0869 and adapter are unchanged. Offline paired cases (`data/dev-lite/realism-20261121/model-paired-cases.mjs`) scored a routine BDT 85,000 claim 0.026–0.067 (no additional model review) and an out-of-range BDT 190,000 claim 0.0885 (review recommended). Amounts now display as `BDT 150,000.00`, and the forms and OCR accept international (150,000.00) or lakh (1,50,000.00) grouping. |
| F25 | Medical cover rules R1–R3: cover could start in the past, before the first premium, or for a stay that began before the policy was bought (F22). | Source review, unit-level, and synthetic one-host live LevelDB dev-lite | Fixed in chaincode (`cover_rules.go`). R1: a medical policy starts between the ledger's Asia/Dhaka date and 60 days ahead, for at most one year. R2: cover starts on the later of the start date and the date the first premium settles (`coverStartDate`); the first premium is accepted until the start date plus the grace period, after which the offer expires. R3: the stay's admission date must be on or after cover start, after the waiting period, and within a paid installment. Live, a backdated purchase returned 409 "never backdated". |
| F26 | Medical cover rules R4–R5: a lapsed policy could pay its missed installments and be covered again, including for the uncovered gap, and the Insurer could move a LAPSED policy back to GRACE with an earlier as-of date. | Source review, unit-level | Fixed in chaincode. An installment paid within grace keeps cover continuous. After due date plus grace, payment is refused. `ReinstatePolicy` (Insurer only, a hashed reason) restarts a lapsed medical policy from today: the reinstatement premium is accepted only on that day, the gap stays uncovered, waiting periods restart, active mandates are re-dated and earlier open collections are SUPERSEDED. `AdvancePolicyLifecycle` allows only expiry out of LAPSED. Ten Go tests cover R1–R6; the live 15-day grace and reinstatement path needs ledger time to pass and was unit-tested only. |
| F27 | Medical cover rule R6: an EFT mandate needed an ACTIVE policy, so the first premium had to be paid by OTP. | Source review, unit-level, and synthetic one-host live LevelDB dev-lite | Fixed. A PENDING_PAYMENT medical policy can set up a mandate whose first debit is the start date. A bounced first debit can be retried until the offer expires. Live, two EFT-first policies were collected and bounced, and the bounced one stayed PENDING_PAYMENT until the retry completed. |
| F28 | Cancelling a policy left its mandates ACTIVE and its collections open, so the Bank could still debit a cancelled policy. A policy could also hold two active mandates and be debited twice for one installment. | Source review, unit-level | Fixed. `endPolicyDebits` supersedes open collections and cancels mandates on cancellation and on a paid death or surrender benefit. Lapse and expiry supersede queued collections. `RequestBankMandate` refuses a second PENDING or ACTIVE mandate, and scheduling skips cancelled, expired or out-of-time policies. |
| F29 | Terminal benefits: a policy could pay more than one death, surrender or maturity benefit and stay ACTIVE after a paid death benefit. | Source review, unit-level | Fixed. A policy holds at most one non-rejected benefit request. A paid death or surrender benefit cancels the policy with its cancellation date and ends its debits. The benefit payout still debits insurer funds without crediting a beneficiary account, because the beneficiary is paid outside the demo Bank. |
| F30 | Several dates could be in the future: hospital episode admission, discharge, service and invoice dates, a generic claim's incident date and a benefit event date. Partner agreement checks also used the UTC date rather than the Asia/Dhaka business date. | Source review, unit-level | Fixed. Each refuses a date later than the ledger business date, and `requireActivePartner` uses `ledgerBusinessDate`. |
| F31 | A cancelled policy refused every claim, even for treatment completed before the cancellation. | Source review, unit-level | Fixed. Cancellation now records `cancelledDate`. Treatment whose admission and service dates fall before that date remains claimable within the paid period; policies cancelled before sequence 7 have no date and keep refusing. |

Not changed and still open: there is no pro-rata premium refund on cancellation; waiting periods do not vary by cause (the rehearsal uses a 0-day "Accident and Emergency Care" package); the generic Oracle API still allows 1–9 assignees while the workflow selects 2-of-3; Fabric keeps only the last event of a transaction. The older general workflow specs (`fabric-workflow.spec.ts` and others) keep their small amounts and backdated dates because they target preserved channels and archived evidence; they were not re-run against sequence 7.
